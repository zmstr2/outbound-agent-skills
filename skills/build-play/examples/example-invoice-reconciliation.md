# Invoice reconciliation play (EXAMPLE, fictional)

We sell "Ledgerly" (fictional) to finance teams at mid-sized companies, to match supplier invoices to purchase orders automatically instead of by hand or in an ERP's basic matching module.

Sources: fictional. In a real play, link your deck, call notes, reviews and the source page of every statistic. This example's prose uses British spelling; the email examples follow the American-spelling default.

## Part A · Research brief

### Who fits
Companies with 500 to 5,000 staff and a finance team of 10 to 80, paying thousands of supplier invoices a month, usually after growth or an acquisition. Fits worse: very small firms (low volume) and firms already on a full procure-to-pay suite.

### Qualifying signal
Two searches: (1) `"<company>" acquisition OR "ERP migration" OR "finance transformation"` for the last 24 months; (2) accounts payable (AP) job ads at the company that mention spreadsheets, backlog or multiple ledgers. Either one counts.

### Current tool or approach
The ERP's built-in matching module, a procure-to-pay suite, an outsourced AP provider, or spreadsheets.

### Profile fields
- ERP: <system>. Note any migration under way.

### Company evidence
- An acquisition or merger that added entities, suppliers or ledgers → Finance change evidence
- ERP or finance system migration, announced or under way → Company profile
- Statements about close speed, audit findings or restatements → Finance change evidence
- News and events from the last 24 months: acquisitions, new CFO, finance transformation, audit issues, cost programmes → News and events

### Job ads
- AP clerk, AP analyst, reconciliation analyst, finance operations or shared-services roles that mention manual matching, spreadsheets or backlog.
- Finance systems or transformation roles.

### Contacts to find
1. CFO or VP Finance. Persona: economic buyer.
2. Financial controller or head of finance operations. Persona: champion.
3. AP manager or shared-services lead, if visible. Persona: user.

**Contact topics:** month-end close, automation, finance transformation, AP backlog.

**Background to note:** did they lead an ERP migration or shared-services build.

**Contact details to collect:** work email only.

**Treat as sensitive** (tagged in the dossier, never used in emails): layoffs or cuts to named finance teams, audit failures not yet public.

### Claims to check on the source page
- The ERP and any migration date.
- A new CFO or controller and their start date.

### Dossier sections
- **After Company profile:** `## Finance change evidence`, IDs F1, F2…, columns `ID | Finding | Published | Source`.

## Part B · Messaging

### Pattern and fit
Pattern: say how much has changed in finance recently (acquisitions, new systems) and how invoices are matched today. Fit: strongest for firms that grew by acquisition; for single-entity firms, lean on close speed and audit risk rather than integration.

### Where we win and lose
We win against any incumbent on:
- **Matching across entities:** one rule set after an acquisition instead of one per ledger.
- **Exceptions in one queue:** the team clears odd invoices once instead of chasing by email.
- **Setup in weeks, not a project.**

| Incumbent | Typical use | Poke | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ERP matching module** | Two-way match on a single ledger | Breaks across entities; exceptions end up in spreadsheets | Basic single-ledger matching (it does this fine) |
| **Procure-to-pay suite** | Full purchasing workflow | Heavy to change; slow to add acquired entities | Approval workflows and supplier portals |
| **No tool** | Spreadsheets and email | Month-end backlog; duplicate payments | none |

**Naming the incumbent in an email:** never. Say "your current matching setup".

### Core pains
| Core pain | Meaning | Angles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Slow close** | Matching backlog delays month-end | 1, 2 |
| **Money lost** | Duplicate or wrong payments | 3 |

**Binding constraint example:** an AP hiring push is a side effect (more headcount; don't poke it). The binding constraint is that month-end close can't start until exceptions clear; poke that.

**Best signals:** AP job ads saying "reconcile in Excel", "month-end backlog", "multiple ledgers", or a controller talking about integrating acquired entities.

### Personas in this play
- **Economic buyer (CFO, VP Finance).** Role in the deal: signs off. Email them? Yes. Measured on: close speed, audit cleanliness, cost per invoice. How to pitch: money and risk. Pains in this play: late closes, audit questions, duplicate payments.
- **Champion (controller, head of finance ops).** Role in the deal: owns close and carries the case. Email them? Yes, best first contact. Measured on: close timeline, controls, team workload. How to pitch: the pain of the work. Pains in this play: backlog, exceptions by email, onboarding acquired entities.
- **User (AP manager, analyst).** Role in the deal: does the matching. Email them? Rarely; use their pain as evidence. Measured on: accuracy and speed. How to pitch: their time. Pains in this play: re-keying, chasing approvals, spreadsheet matching.

### Angle bank
Default angles: 1, 2, 3.
1. **Close waits on matching** (slow close). Pain: month-end stalls on unmatched invoices. Signals: AP roles mentioning backlog or spreadsheets; close problems in news. Name: days added to close; finance staying late. Value: invoices matched as they arrive, so close starts clean. Strong vs ERP module, no tool.
2. **Acquired entities, new rules** (slow close). Pain: each acquired ledger has its own matching. Signals: acquisition in the last 24 months; multiple ERPs. Name: weeks before a new entity is on the same process. Value: one rule set across entities. Strong vs ERP module.
3. **Paying twice** (money lost). Pain: duplicate or mismatched payments slip through. Signals: audit findings, restated costs, multiple ledgers. Name: money out the door before anyone notices. Value: a check on the cause in their own data. Strong vs no tool.
