# Interview guide

Ask in short rounds (3 to 5 questions), not one long form. Skip anything the user's files or tools already answer. If they say "I don't know", mark `TODO` and move on.

## Look first, then ask
Before or between rounds, look for answers the user already has and show what you found so they can correct it:
- **Local files:** decks, battlecards, one-pagers, call notes, past emails that got replies.
- **Connected apps** (CRM, call recorder, docs, email, chat): closed-won deals, lost-deal reasons, objections, customer quotes, winning subject lines. Anonymise people and customers before writing anything into the play.
- **Their website:** positioning, case studies, pricing, comparison pages.

## Round 1 · The offer and who it's for
1. In one or two sentences, what do you sell, and what does the buyer get?
2. Which kind of company is this for? (size, industry, region, poor fits)
3. What event or change makes them need it now? (a hire, a restructure, a launch, a regulation, a cost target)

## Round 2 · The alternative
4. What do these companies use today instead of you? (named tools, consultancies, spreadsheets, nothing)
5. Where are you clearly better? Where do they beat you, and what should we never poke?
6. How do accounts describe the problem or the current tool in their own words (reviews, job ads, calls)?

## Round 3 · People
7. Who buys, who champions, who does the hands-on work, who can block? Titles for each.
8. What is each measured on, and what keeps them up at night?
9. Who should we email first, and who should we never email?

## Round 4 · Proof and limits
10. Which numbers can we use publicly, and where's the source? Which customers have agreed to be mentioned, or described anonymously?
11. Words or claims to avoid (legal, brand, past complaints)?
12. What might research turn up that must never appear in an email (layoffs, legal trouble)?

## Market research
- The incumbent's site, reviews (G2, Capterra, Reddit, forums), and job ads that name it.
- Buyer language: how target titles describe the problem in posts, talks, job ads and analyst reports.
- Triggers: public events that show an account is entering the pain (funding, leadership change, hiring pattern, regulation, launch).
- Record the link and date for every statistic.
